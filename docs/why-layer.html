The Why Layer

The arithmetic behind “why”, written in SQL rather than by a model. Each primitive is hand-written, read-only and deterministic; the model’s only job is choosing which one to call and filling in a name.

The campaign spine

Eight of the eleven start from the same three-table join. A datasheet belongs to a planner entry, which belongs to a request — and the request is what carries the TCO and the product name. Listed once here rather than repeated on every card below.

datasheet→ planner_entry_id planner_entries→ test_request_id iec_emc_requests

The two axes, which nothing here mixes

“Failed” is ambiguous in this building, and answering the wrong sense confidently is worse than saying nothing. emc_reason_code.family is what keeps them apart.

family = test_failure

datasheet.failure_reason_code

Why the unit failed a standard. CE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED, ESD_LOCKUP, SURGE_DAMAGE. Read by failure_detail, failure_modes, cohort.

family = review_rejection

datasheet_status_history.reason_code

Why the record was sent back by a peer reviewer. MISSING_PHOTO, CAL_EXPIRED, UNIT_ERROR. Read by review_history and rejection_modes.

Tables and returned columns above were extracted from the executing SQL and from live results, not from documentation.

Arguments are sanitised before any primitive runs: a malformed TCO is dropped, a placeholder product name (“ALL”) is dropped, and a product supplied alongside a TCO is dropped because a TCO already names exactly one job. An empty result from two or more combined filters says they may disagree rather than reporting an absence.
